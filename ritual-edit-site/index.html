Skin notes · 5 min

The five-minute morning edit

A small, repeatable routine can make more sense than a crowded bathroom shelf.

Open the note +

Your morning routine does not need to be complicated. Start with a gentle cleanse or a water rinse if that is enough for your skin, add a comfortable moisturiser, then finish with broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher.

Keep the order simple

  • Cleanse without leaving skin tight or stripped.
  • Moisturise according to how your skin feels.
  • Apply sunscreen as directed and reapply when outdoors.

Build around comfort and consistency. For further reading, visit the Skin Hub and see an example of SPF 50 sunscreen.

Skin notes · 7 min

Breakouts are information, not a verdict

Think about recurring blemishes without scrubbing, picking or over-correcting.

Open the note +

Acne can involve blackheads, whiteheads, inflamed spots and deeper painful bumps. It is influenced by several factors and is not proof that skin is unclean. Harsh cleansing and squeezing can worsen irritation and marks.

Return to the basics

Choose gentle cleansing, a suitable moisturiser and sunscreen. Introduce one targeted active at a time. Painful, scarring or persistent acne deserves a dermatologist's advice.

Skin notes · 6 min

Uneven tone: think protection first

A careful look at sun exposure, post-blemish marks and patience in tone care.

Open the note +

Uneven tone can have different causes, including sun exposure, post-inflammatory marks and hormonal factors. A routine cannot be chosen well until the likely cause is understood. Sun protection is a sensible foundation for daytime routines.

Keep expectations realistic

Use broad-spectrum sunscreen, avoid picking at blemishes and introduce brightening products gradually. New, changing or concerning pigmented spots should be assessed by a clinician.

Hair notes · 6 min

When more hair ends up in the brush

Understand the growth cycle and why a sudden change should not be self-diagnosed.

Open the note +

Hair naturally cycles through growing, transitioning and resting phases. Stress, illness, hormonal changes, genetics, medicines and nutritional factors can affect shedding. Timing and pattern help a clinician assess the cause.

Track the pattern

  • Note when shedding began and whether it is diffuse or patchy.
  • Notice scalp pain, itching, scaling or redness.
  • Avoid starting several supplements or aggressive treatments without advice.

For broader context, visit the Hair Hub and its overview of hair-loss management topics. Sudden or persistent loss should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Hair notes · 5 min

The scalp reset: flakes, itch and comfort

Flaking has more than one possible cause. Start by noticing the pattern.

Open the note +

Flakes may be associated with dryness, dandruff, seborrhoeic dermatitis, eczema, psoriasis or irritation from a hair product. Itch and redness overlap, so appearance alone cannot confirm the cause.

Gentle habits first

Use a shampoo that suits your scalp and follow its directions. Avoid scratching or scraping scale. Persistent itch, sores, thick scale, pain or spreading redness are reasons to seek assessment.

Hair notes · 5 min

Frizz has a history

Heat, friction, humidity and chemical processing can all change how hair behaves.

Open the note +

Hair texture is individual, but damaged or lifted cuticles can make strands feel rough and tangle more easily. Frequent heat styling, bleaching and friction may contribute.

Make the routine kinder

  • Condition mid-lengths and ends.
  • Detangle gently and avoid rough towel rubbing.
  • Reduce repeated high heat and tight styles.
  • Use styling products for manageability, not as a substitute for repairing damage.

Ingredient index · 6 min

Niacinamide, without the hype

A plain-language look at the vitamin B3 ingredient in many skincare formulas.

Open the note +

Niacinamide is a form of vitamin B3 used in topical skincare. It features in formulas intended to support the skin barrier and improve the appearance of uneven tone. Results depend on formula, concentration, regular use and individual tolerance.

Read the whole formula

Do not judge a product by one ingredient or a bigger percentage. Introduce one new product at a time, follow label directions and stop if irritation persists.

Ingredient index · 7 min

Exfoliation is not a competition

Understand acids, frequency and why professional peels belong in trained hands.

Open the note +

Exfoliating acids are used in cosmetic products, but their effect depends on the acid, formula, pH, concentration and frequency. Using more or using it more often can increase irritation rather than improve results.

Know the difference

Professional chemical peels are not everyday exfoliants. Improper use can cause burns, pigment changes, infection or scarring. Selection and treatment should be handled by appropriately trained professionals.

Hair notes · 6 min

Hair growth products: ask the right question

Cosmetic care can support a routine, but different types of hair loss need different answers.

Open the note +

Hair growth is influenced by genetics, hormones, age, health and the hair cycle. A cosmetic serum may be part of personal care, but it should not be assumed to treat an underlying medical cause of hair loss.

When to seek advice

Sudden shedding, patchy loss, scalp inflammation or progressive thinning deserves a clinician's assessment. Avoid relying on dramatic product claims or supplements unless a deficiency is confirmed.
