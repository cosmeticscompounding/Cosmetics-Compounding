BRIEF 01 / SKIN

Is it dryness, a breakout, or a change in tone?

Start by describing the pattern instead of reaching for a stronger product. Skin concerns can overlap, and the next step depends on how long it has been happening and what else has changed.

Notice

Stinging, tightness or flaking after a wash can be a reason to review cleansing and active ingredients.

Try first

Keep a gentle cleanser, a comfortable moisturiser and daytime sun protection as routine anchors.

Get advice

Persistent pain, swelling, scarring or a new changing pigmented spot deserves qualified assessment.

Desk 04 — Ingredient counter

Four ingredients. Four different jobs.

Ingredients are not interchangeable. The complete formula, its directions and your tolerance are more useful than a trending percentage or a bold front-label claim.

IngredientWhat it isWhat to keep in mindRelated reading

Niacinamide

A form of vitamin B3 used in topical skincare formulas.

May support barrier function and the appearance of uneven tone; the whole formula matters.

Serum example ↗

Hyaluronic acid

A humectant used to attract and hold water in a formula.

Texture, base formula and the moisturiser used alongside it affect the experience.

Serum example ↗

Retinol

A topical retinoid in the vitamin A family.

Can irritate; introduction and frequency matter. Follow label guidance and seek advice for individual circumstances.

Serum example ↗

Exfoliating acids

A broad group of ingredients used to loosen surface cell connections.

Strength, pH and frequency vary. Professional peels are not equivalent to everyday home exfoliation.

Desk 05 — The weekly check-in

One week / one small plan

What can you repeat without overthinking it?

Choose a few routine anchors, keep new products separate, and make a note if irritation or an unexpected change appears. This is a simple checklist, not a medical tracker.

Useful rule: do not introduce several new actives at the same time. Consistency makes it easier to identify what suits you.
